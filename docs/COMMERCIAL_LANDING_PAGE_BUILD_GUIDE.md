# Commercial Landing Page Build Guide

## Status

Reusable build guide for GhostTown and future Zayas House commercial product pages.

This guide captures the useful offer-building principles from the Hormozi-based work while removing the old visual restriction that forced GhostTown toward image-light, card-heavy presentation.

**Superseded rule:** `evidence-first frontier modernism` is not a governing visual constraint for GhostTown commercial UI. Evidence integrity remains mandatory; visual expression is open.

## 1. Governing rule

A commercial page exists to help a customer quickly understand:

1. What is this?
2. Is it for me?
3. What problem does it solve?
4. What will I get?
5. Why should I believe it?
6. What do I do next?

Truth is mandatory. A specific visual style is not.

Use any visual medium—photography, product UI, illustration, diagrams, motion, 3D, video, data visualization, or generated imagery—when it improves understanding, desire, trust, usability, or conversion.

Do not use generic, misleading, irrelevant, low-quality, or rights-unclear imagery.

## 2. Plain-language standard

Write for fast understanding, not for sophistication.

Target the clarity of language a child can follow even when the product itself is complex.

Prefer:

- short words;
- short sentences;
- one idea per paragraph;
- concrete nouns and verbs;
- customer language;
- visible outcomes;
- clear next actions.

Avoid:

- jargon when a common word works;
- stacked abstractions;
- technical implementation details in the offer name;
- vague AI language;
- long feature lists before the customer understands the outcome.

Examples:

| Harder copy | Better copy |
|---|---|
| Pressure-test demand, buyer access, and willingness to pay. | See if people want it before you build it. |
| Customer-access research | Find where your customers are and how to reach them. |
| Positioning and offer strategy | Know what to say, who it is for, and what to charge. |
| Personalized Cloudflare Launch Site Starter | Custom Launch Website |

## 3. Page sequence

Long is acceptable. Dense is not.

Use one dominant idea per section. Let each section answer one customer question before asking the next.

Recommended sequence:

1. **Outcome-led hero** — who this is for, what changes, primary CTA.
2. **Visual transformation** — show the customer journey, not a decorative image.
3. **Problem** — what goes wrong today.
4. **Stakes** — what delay, confusion, or the wrong path costs.
5. **Desired future** — the simple outcome the customer wants.
6. **Product magic moment** — show the product producing the outcome.
7. **Simple mechanism** — explain how it works in a few steps.
8. **Proof / truth boundary** — show why the method is credible without fake proof.
9. **Free or low-friction first step** — let the customer experience value.
10. **Paid transformation** — explain what the paid product adds.
11. **Value stack** — show concrete finished outputs before price.
12. **Effort reduction** — explain what work is already done or made easier.
13. **Objections** — answer reasonable doubts in customer language.
14. **Risk reversal** — use only the real published guarantee or refund policy.
15. **Price** — reveal price after value is understood.
16. **FAQ** — clean up remaining friction.
17. **Final CTA** — restate the outcome and the next action.

Do not force every page to have exactly 17 blocks. Preserve the sequence of questions even when blocks are combined.

## 4. Hero story rule

When the customer problem is emotional or easy to recognize visually, use the hero media to tell the whole story in a few seconds.

For GhostTown, the founder journey is:

1. You get an idea.
2. You start building.
3. You keep adding, fixing, and polishing.
4. People can look without buying.
5. Good feedback does not always mean sales.
6. Months can pass before you know if people really want it.
7. GhostTown helps you decide if it is worth building.

The hero sequence may auto-advance, but it must:

- provide manual previous/next controls;
- provide a pause control;
- respect `prefers-reduced-motion`;
- avoid blocking access to the CTA;
- use short captions;
- pause longer on the final product-payoff frame.

## 5. Relatability and imagery

People should be able to see themselves in the page.

Use a believable range of builders when relevant to the market:

- women and men;
- different racial and ethnic backgrounds;
- younger and older builders;
- solo founders and partners;
- office, home, side-project, and small-business contexts.

Do not use demographic variety as decoration. Each image should advance the customer story.

## 6. Real customer/founder language

Real public complaints can improve credibility when used carefully.

Rules:

- verify the source;
- quote only a short passage;
- name the public source when appropriate;
- never imply the person in an illustrative photo is the quoted founder;
- label illustrative photography when a quote is overlaid;
- do not invent testimonials;
- do not convert a paraphrase into quotation marks.

Use only a few quotes. The rest of the story should use simple captions.

## 7. Value before price

Before showing the price, the customer should understand:

- the outcome;
- the work they avoid;
- the work they receive;
- the finished assets;
- how those assets connect to the outcome;
- what the first useful action looks like.

For GhostTown, the $97 paid value is not “a PDF.” It is the 30-Day Evidence Sprint: the plan, daily next steps, research, messages, evidence ledger, and decision rules that tell the customer what to test and what the result means. A live customer-facing site is not part of the $97 Sprint.

## 8. Name deliverables by customer value

Do not expose implementation infrastructure in the customer-facing product name unless it is itself valuable to the buyer.

Bad:

> Personalized Cloudflare Launch Site Starter

Better:

> **Get Me Live — $297 one-time**
>
> Turn the tested offer from your Sprint into a live customer-facing business you can send to real customers.

This is a separate product from the $97 30-Day Sprint. The internal implementation can reuse Cloudflare and the canonical `Launch Site` model, but customer-facing naming should describe the outcome rather than the infrastructure.

### GhostTown commercial product boundary

| Step | Customer value | Product boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Evidence Scan + Verdict | See what outside market signs exist, what is still unproven, and whether the idea is clear enough to test. |
| $97 | 30-Day Evidence Sprint | Decide **what to test**, what to do next, and what evidence means keep going, revise, pause, or stop. No live website, domain, publishing, or payment setup is included. |
| $297 one-time | Get Me Live | Turn the Sprint test into a live customer-facing business with guided setup, lead capture, tracking, publishing, and release proof. |
| Separate upsell | Story Studio | Help more people find the test. Distribution is separate from deciding what to test and separate from manufacturing the live page. |

## 9. Truth and offer safety

Hormozi-style offer design may improve:

- outcome clarity;
- value perception;
- objection handling;
- page order;
- effort reduction;
- uncertainty reduction;
- CTA clarity.

It must not create:

- fabricated proof;
- fake testimonials;
- false urgency;
- fake scarcity;
- unsupported guarantees;
- invented results;
- exaggerated product capabilities.

GhostTown remains a decision aid. It does not guarantee validation, customers, sales, revenue, product-market fit, or business success.

## 10. Conversion checks

Before approving a page, ask:

- Can a first-time visitor explain the product after 10 seconds?
- Is the main outcome visible before the mechanism?
- Is the primary CTA obvious?
- Does every section have one job?
- Is there enough visual pacing between ideas?
- Is the product shown, not only described?
- Is the paid value visible before the price?
- Are objections answered in plain language?
- Are technical terms translated into customer value?
- Does mobile preserve the same story and CTA hierarchy?

## 11. Accessibility and motion

World-class commercial UI must remain usable.

- one meaningful `h1`;
- semantic heading order;
- real buttons and links;
- visible keyboard focus;
- at least 44px direct-touch controls;
- no status conveyed by color alone;
- reduced-motion support;
- no auto-advancing content that cannot be paused;
- no sticky CTA that hides focused content;
- no page-level horizontal scrolling at phone widths.

## 12. GhostTown reference implementation

The GhostTown commercial acquisition branch is the first reference implementation of this guide.

The commercial story should remain:

> **Don’t build the wrong idea.**
>
> Test your idea first. See if people want it. Then decide if it’s worth building.

The paid continuation should remain subordinate to the free verdict until the customer understands the first value exchange.

# Story Studio Baseline 001 — Production, Experimentation & Commercial Learning Operating Contract

**Status:** Governing Story Studio commercial-learning contract for GhostTown integration  
**Applies to:** GhostTown + Story Studio + `roadmap:autopilot`

## 1. Governing definition

Story Studio is both:

> **CONTENT FACTORY + EXPERIMENTAL ACQUISITION SYSTEM**

The Content Factory produces shows, characters, masters, shorts, derivatives, localized variants, platform variants, and global distribution.

The Acquisition Experiment System tests hooks, angles, formats, CTAs, audiences, markets, platforms, attribution, performance, and revenue.

The Learning Engine identifies winners and patterns, mutates successful concepts, reallocates distribution, and deliberately preserves continued exploration.

```text
TRUSTED SOURCE MATERIAL
        ↓
CONTENT FACTORY
        ↓
CREATIVES + VARIANTS
        ↓
DISTRIBUTION
        ↓
ACQUISITION EXPERIMENT ENGINE
        ↓
PERFORMANCE + ATTRIBUTION
        ↓
LEARNING ENGINE
        ↓
WINNER IDENTIFICATION
        ↓
MUTATION + ROUTING + REALLOCATION
        ↓
CONTENT FACTORY
```

Mission:

> **Truth → stories → measurable business outcomes.**

## 2. Exploration before exploitation

The first large production run exists to discover winners, not to confirm assumptions we already hold.

```text
PHASE 1 — EXPLORATION
Generate broadly.
Measure rigorously.
Learn aggressively.
Do not prematurely optimize.
```

After sufficient evidence:

```text
PHASE 2 — OPTIMIZATION
Exploit demonstrated winners.
Mutate winners.
Allocate more distribution to winners.
Preserve controlled exploration.
```

Post-Baseline operating allocation is approximately:

```text
80% exploitation
20% continued exploration
```

Small slices prove **machine readiness**, not commercial winner validity.

## 4. Slice A — Gate 39

```text
1 verdict × 2 creatives × 1 platform
```

Must prove source ingestion, treatment generation, rendering, publication, publication receipt, attribution continuity, performance observation, quality floor, and experiment-ledger integrity.

## 5. Slice B — Gate 40

```text
3 verdicts × several treatments × 2 platforms
```

Must prove independent verdict execution, treatment identity, platform-specific variants, publication lineage, normalized metrics without collapsing platform identity, and isolated failure handling.

## 6. Slice C — Gate 41

```text
10 verdicts × 10 treatments = 100 master creatives
```

Purpose: prove machine readiness at meaningful batch scale.

Must prove batch orchestration, treatment registry, creative lineage, render/publish reliability, quality enforcement, market/platform variants, analytics ingestion, experiment ledger, recovery, and attribution continuity.

Slice C does not attempt to prove the commercial winner.

## 7. Mandatory Show/Treatment Portfolio Review

Before finalizing the ten treatments used for Slice C and Baseline 001, explicitly review the current GhostTown show portfolio:

1. GhostTown Verdict
2. Founder Court
3. Build It / Forget It
4. Founder Intervention / Stop Hurting Your Startup
5. GhostTown Rescue

Every show receives exactly one classification:

```text
KEEP
EVOLVE
REPLACE
MERGE

```

Evaluate against the current GhostTown promise:

```text
Should you test this?
What is the biggest uncertainty?
How do you falsify it cheaply?
Who is the first buyer?
What should you do today?
```

Current portfolio direction to test:

- **GhostTown Verdict — KEEP / EVOLVE** toward `Opportunity → Risk → Unknown → Killer Test → Verdict → First Move`.
- **Founder Court — KEEP** because conflict naturally communicates evidence and uncertainty.
- **Build It / Forget It — REASSESS / EVOLVE** because it may overlap with GhostTown Verdict.
- **Founder Intervention — KEEP** for founder-error education beyond individual verdicts.
- **GhostTown Rescue — KEEP** for transformation from weak idea to stronger customer/offer/channel/scope.

Candidate acquisition treatments include:

```text
Killer Assumption
First Customer
The $100 Test
Idea Autopsy
Customer Says No
```

The ten Baseline treatments do **not** need to equal ten permanent television-style shows. They must be ten genuinely differentiated acquisition treatments.

## 10. Experimental unit and lineage

Canonical hierarchy:

```text
VERDICT
    ↓
CREATIVE FAMILY
    ↓
CREATIVE
    ↓
MARKET VARIANT
    ↓
PLATFORM VARIANT
    ↓
PUBLICATION
    ↓
PERFORMANCE OBSERVATION
    ↓
ATTRIBUTED GHOSTTOWN BEHAVIOR
```

A creative distributed across multiple platforms remains one creative with multiple observations. Locale and market identity must remain explicit.

## 11. Experiment dimensions

Preserve metadata for:

```text
SHOW / TREATMENT
× VERDICT CHARACTERISTICS
× HOOK
× ANGLE
× PLATFORM
× MARKET
× CTA
× DURATION
× PRESENTATION
```

Subsequent experiments may also vary character, voice, music, opening visual, pacing, localization, and platform treatment.

The objective is not merely the show with the highest view count. The more valuable finding may be a routing relationship between verdict characteristics and treatment performance.

## 12. Observation windows — Gate 43

Every eligible publication generates immutable or append-only observations at:

```text
1 hour
24 hours
72 hours
7 days
```

Final Baseline decisions should not pretend incomplete windows are complete evidence.

## 13. Evidence classes

### Distribution

Impressions, reach, views, qualified views, watch time, completion, retention, engagement, shares, saves.

### Acquisition

Profile visits, link clicks, qualified clicks, landing sessions, GhostTown tests started, verdicts completed.

### Commercial

Checkout initiated, paid Blueprint purchase, attributed revenue, revenue per creative, conversion rate, CAC where spend exists.

Views are not the final objective.

## 14. Winner Engine — Gate 44

Classify more than absolute winners:

```text
GLOBAL WINNER
SEGMENT WINNER
MARKET WINNER
PLATFORM WINNER
VERDICT-TYPE WINNER
COMMERCIAL WINNER
ATTENTION-ONLY WINNER
UNDERPERFORMER
INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE
```

A technically valid video can lose the experiment. A high-view creative can fail commercially.

## 15. 80/20 exploitation/exploration

After Baseline 001, allocate approximately 80% of capacity to known or predicted winners and approximately 20% to continued exploration.

Exploration includes novel hooks, new treatments, new shows, new markets, presentation styles, CTAs, voices, durations, and localizations.

## 16. Winner mutation

```text
PARENT WINNER
↓
hook / opening / duration / CTA / voice / localization / visual / pacing / platform mutations
↓
PUBLICATION
↓
OBSERVATION
↓
BETTER / WORSE / DIFFERENT SEGMENT
```

Mutation is an experiment, not merely additional content production.

## 17. Predictive routing

Long-term routing:

```text
NEW GHOSTTOWN VERDICT
↓
extract verdict characteristics
↓
query historical performance
↓
predict best treatment combinations
↓
allocate production/distribution budget
↓
generate
↓
publish
↓
observe
↓
learn
```

The experiment corpus becomes a production-allocation decision asset.

## 18. Baseline decision report — Gate 45

The machine-readable decision report must include experiment accounting, performance findings, treatment × verdict interactions, platform/market/locale/hook/CTA/duration/presentation effects, GhostTown funnel behavior, purchases/revenue, and explicit `EVIDENCE → INTERPRETATION → DECISION → PRODUCTION CHANGE → NEXT EXPERIMENT` chains.

Evidence and inference must remain distinguishable.

## 19. Commercial proof — Gate 46

The commercial milestone is:

```text
FIRST ATTRIBUTABLE STRANGER
→ $97 PURCHASE
```

followed by repeatable acquisition economics.

This is not permission to operate the Content Factory. The large exploration corpus exists partly to discover the strategies capable of producing this result.

This asks: **Can we trust the machine to conduct the experiment?** It does not ask whether a winner is already known.

## 21. Non-negotiable operating rules

1. Do not optimize treatments away before Baseline 001 is complete.
2. Production success and experiment success are different.
3. Never destroy lineage.
4. Global distribution remains part of the design.
5. Views are not the final objective.
6. Preserve exploration after winners emerge.
7. Winners are conditional until proven otherwise.
8. Learning must be capable of changing future production.

## 22. Governing decision

Proceed with the 1,000-creative Baseline 001 ambition after machine-readiness calibration.

The production-learning path is:

> **100 verdicts × 10 treatments → 1,000 controlled master creatives → observe → learn → exploit → explore → mutate → route → attribute revenue.**
